# Tehara: Level Designs (draft for review)

This is the level plan we fill out **before building anything**. It has three parts:

- **Part A**: shared rules every level follows.
- **Part B**: full designs for **version 1**: World 1 *The Road to Pesach* (10 levels) and the *Chanukah* pack (6 levels).
- **Part C**: an idea bank of about 40 more levels for the later worlds, in short form. Each one gets a full design when its world is next.

Every rule below traces to a source that was read on Sefaria (Rambam's Sefer Taharah first). Sources marked **[to verify]** are from the commentaries or from memory and must be checked before that level ships. The research files in `docs/research/` have the full quotes.

# Part A: shared rules

## A1. The hero
**Eli** (אלי), a young Kohen (כהן), about bar-mitzvah age, from a village in the Galil. In World 1 he travels up to Yerushalayim to bring the Korban Pesach (קרבן פסח) with his family. Bright red-brown hair, a white linen tunic and a white linen cap, like a Kohen. (The real priestly garments [בגדי כהונה] are worn only in the Mikdash, on the day of service and never with the avnet outside the Avodah (Rambam, Klei HaMikdash 8:11-12; Yoma 69a), so on the road he wears everyday white clothes in the same style.) He walks by himself when the path is clear, and stops and waits when it isn't.

Supporting cast:
- **Gardener Gad** (גד הגנן), a Yisrael with a big straw hat and a leaf blower.
- **Grandpa Zevulun** (סבא זבולון), a village elder who knows where the old graves are.
- **Miriam** (מרים), Eli's older sister, who carries the family's terumah (תרומה) basket.
- **Batya** (בתיה), a Beit Din (בית דין) messenger with a bucket of lime.

## A2. Language
- **English version:** Hebrew terms in square brackets the first time they appear on a screen, e.g. *"Eli is a Kohen [כהן]. He may not become impure [טמא] from a dead body [מת]."* Later on the same screen, the Hebrew-letter word alone is fine once kids know it.
- **Hebrew version:** the same text in plain Hebrew.
- Source lines on the Why? card are always in the original Hebrew, with an English translation in the English version.

## A3. How tumah looks (from the design plan)
- **Tahor:** normal colors.
- **Av HaTumah [אב הטומאה]:** purple glow with an "Av" badge.
- **Rishon / Sheni / Shlishi [ראשון / שני / שלישי]:** a softer glow with a 1 / 2 / 3 badge.
- **Ohel [אהל]:** everything under one roof with a met gets a light purple tint.
- **Cartoon skeletons and bones:** friendly, round, never scary. A grave is a stone mound or a carved door in a hillside. A whole met is always a grave or a closed cave, never a body lying out.

## A4. Win, fail, stars
- **Win:** Eli reaches the goal tahor. He waves and confetti falls.
- **Fail:** Eli glows purple and the scene **rewinds one second** with a soft "whoosh". No screaming, no "you died". A one-line hint explains *which rule* was broken (e.g. "A barley-sized bone [עצם כשעורה] makes you tamei if you move it!").
- **Stars:** ★ for finishing with a hint, ★★ for finishing without a hint, ★★★ for no hint and no mistakes. Where a level has a "clever" solution (like knocking the mouse away in 1-2), ★★★ also needs that solution. It is never just "faster". It always uses a deeper rule.
- **Becoming tamei is not always failure.** In some levels Eli *may* become tamei (e.g. from a sheretz) and the level is still won. The game says so clearly: tumah is a status, not a sin.

## A5. Design guard-rails for every level
1. **Four amot (ארבע אמות):** a Kohen may not come within 4 amot of a met (Rambam Avel 3:13). Paths always pass at a visible distance from any grave or cave with a met.
2. **Only plowed-grave fields** are crossed by blowing. Lost-grave fields are never crossed at all.
3. **No terumah is eaten** after a blowing-crossing in the same story stretch (אין בודקין לתרומה, Oholot 18:4).
4. **Left out:** zav, niddah and similar topics, gentile graves, anything gory.

# Part B: version 1 levels

## World 1: The Road to Pesach (הדרך לפסח)

It's Nisan. Eli's family sets out from the Galil to bring the Korban Pesach in Yerushalayim. Every level is a stop on the road. The world teaches tumat met [טומאת מת] the way a Kohen meets it on a journey: graves, bones, roofs, trees, Beit HaPeras fields and lime markings.

| # | Level | New mechanic | Main rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-1 | Two Paths | Pull a pin | A Kohen doesn't touch a grave or stand over it |
| 1-2 | The Mouse on the Beam | Mikveh + sunset dial | A Kohen *may* become tamei from a sheretz; moving a sheretz with a stick isn't tumah |
| 1-3 | The Bone Bridge | Seesaw / plank | A single bone: walking over it is fine, moving it is not |
| 1-4 | The Gardener's Tree | Prune a branch | Tree branches over a grave are an ohel |
| 1-5 | Stones That Don't Wobble | Tap-test stones; ride the donkey | Crossing a Beit HaPeras: solid stones or a strong animal |
| 1-6 | The Leaf-Blower Field ⭐ | Leaf blower + draw a path | Blow on a Beit HaPeras and walk |
| 1-7 | The Washed-Away Mark | Paint with lime | Mark graves a little wider than the tumah, not much wider |
| 1-8 | Read the White Marks | Tap tiles: tahor or tamei | How to read lime markings |
| 1-9 | Trees or No Trees? | Choose a route | A marked field without trees = lost grave = no crossing |
| 1-10 | The Pilgrims' Path | Find the trodden path | A trodden path in a Beit HaPeras is tahor for the Pesach |

### 1-1 · Two Paths (שתי דרכים)
**Teaches:** a Kohen may not become tamei from a met. That includes touching a grave or standing over it.
**New mechanic:** pull a pin (tutorial).

**Layout** (one screen, portrait):
- Eli stands at the top left, outside his house.
- Two paths lead down to the road at the bottom right.
  - The **short path** goes straight over a low stone mound: an old grave [קבר], with a small carved marker.
  - The **long path** curves around the far side, at a clear distance, but a wooden gate held by a golden pin blocks it.

**Solution:** pull the pin. The gate drops into a bridge, and Eli takes the long path.

**Fail:**
- If the player taps "Go" without pulling the pin, Eli starts down the short path.
- He stops at the edge of the mound, and a thought bubble shows the grave glowing purple: *"A Kohen [כהן] may not step on a grave!"* (He never actually steps on it, even in the fail.)

**Why? card**
> **EN:** Eli is a Kohen [כהן]. The Torah tells a Kohen not to become impure [טמא] from a dead person [מת]. That means not **touching** a grave [קבר], not **carrying** anything from a met, and not being **over** it or under the same roof [אהל] as it. So Eli takes the long way around.
> **HE:** אלי הוא כהן. התורה מצווה על הכהן שלא ייטמא למת: לא לגעת בקבר, לא לשאת, ולא להאהיל עליו או להיות איתו תחת אותו גג. לכן אלי הולך בדרך הארוכה.
>
> *"לְנֶפֶשׁ לֹא יִטַּמָּא בְּעַמָּיו"* (Vayikra 21:1) · *"וְאֶחָד הַנּוֹגֵעַ בְּמֵת אוֹ הַמַּאֲהִיל אוֹ הַנּוֹשֵׂא"* (Rambam, Hilchot Avel 3:1) · A grave conveys tumah by touch and by ohel (Rambam, Tumat Met 2:15)

### 1-2 · The Mouse on the Beam (העכבר שעל הקורה)
**Teaches:**
- A Kohen **may** become tamei from other kinds of tumah. Only tumat met is forbidden to him.
- Mikveh [מקווה] + waiting for sunset [הערב שמש] before eating terumah.
- **★★★:** a sheretz [שרץ] doesn't make you tamei by moving it [משא], only by touching it.

**New mechanics:** mikveh door, sunset dial, a "tool on a pin" (a stick that drops when its pin is pulled).

**Layout** (two rooms, side by side):
- **Room 1:** Eli has to cross to the barn to fetch the family's travel donkey. There are two ways:
  - **(a)** Through an old burial cave [מערת קבורה], dark, with a closed stone door and a small "no Kohanim" sign. The game never lets Eli enter.
  - **(b)** A narrow beam over a ditch. A dead mouse lies right in the middle of the beam (cartoon: X-eyes, legs up).
  - Above the beam, a stick hangs from a golden pin.
- **Room 2:** the village mikveh, with a door held by a pin. In the corner, a sun dial you can turn from afternoon to night. The family's dinner table with a gold-rim terumah plate.

**Solutions:**
- **★ (allowed):** Eli walks the beam and steps over the mouse; his foot touches it. He glows **1** (Rishon). He can still fetch the donkey: becoming tamei from a sheretz is allowed. To eat terumah at dinner, open the mikveh door (Eli dips) and turn the dial past sunset until three stars come out. The glow fades and he sits down to eat.
- **★★★ (clever):** pull the stick's pin first. The stick drops and knocks the mouse off the beam. Eli never touches it and stays tahor. No mikveh needed.

**Fail:**
- Trying the cave: Eli refuses ("Not for a Kohen!").
- Taking the terumah before sunset: the plate shakes "not yet!", with the hint *"After the mikveh, a Kohen waits for the stars before eating terumah."*

**Why? card**
> **EN:** A dead mouse or lizard is a sheretz [שרץ]. If you **touch** it, you become impure [טמא] until evening. A Kohen **is allowed** to become impure this way. The Torah only forbids him tumah from a dead person [מת]. After a mikveh [מקווה], he waits until the stars come out before eating terumah [תרומה].
> Bonus: a sheretz makes you impure by **touch** only, not by moving it. So knocking it away with a stick keeps you pure [טהור]!
> **HE:** עכבר מת הוא שרץ. הנוגע בו נטמא עד הערב. לכהן מותר להיטמא בשרץ, כי התורה אסרה עליו רק טומאת מת. אחרי טבילה הוא מחכה לצאת הכוכבים, ואז אוכל תרומה. ועוד: שרץ מטמא רק במגע ולא במשא, ולכן אם מזיזים אותו במקל, נשארים טהורים!
>
> *"כׇּל־הַנֹּגֵעַ בָּהֶם בְּמֹתָם יִטְמָא עַד־הָעָרֶב"* (Vayikra 11:31) · *"הַשֶּׁרֶץ... מְטַמֵּא אָדָם וְכֵלִים בְּמַגָּע... וְאֵינוֹ מְטַמֵּא בְּמַשָּׂא"* (Rambam, She'ar Avot HaTumot 4:2) · *"וְשֶׁאַף כֹּהֲנִים וּנְזִירִים מֻתָּרִין לְהִתְטַמֵּא בִּשְׁאָר טֻמְאוֹת חוּץ מִטְּמֵא מֵת"* (Rambam, Tumat Okhalin 16:9) · *"אֵין הַטְּמֵאִים אוֹכְלִין בִּתְרוּמָה עַד שֶׁיַּעֲרִיב שִׁמְשָׁן וְיֵצְאוּ שְׁלֹשָׁה כּוֹכָבִים"* (Rambam, Terumot 7:2)

**Guard-rail:** the beam is far from the cave (4 amot).

### 1-3 · The Bone Bridge (גשר העצם)
**Teaches:** a single human bone, even a tiny one the size of a barley grain [עצם כשעורה], makes you tamei if you **touch** it or **move** it, even by pressing a plank it lies on. It does **not** make anything tamei by ohel. Walking right over it is fine.

**New mechanic:** a seesaw plank (it tilts when weight is on one end).

**Layout** (one screen, a ravine):
- A small cartoon bone lies at the bottom of the ravine. A sign from the Beit Din says "a bone was found here".
- Two ways across:
  - **(a)** A **seesaw plank** resting on a rock. The bone has rolled onto its far end. Stepping on the near end would tip it and lift the bone.
  - **(b)** A **fixed log** high up, directly over the bone, but it's lying on the ground. Pull a pin to let a rope swing it into place.

**Solution:** pull the rope's pin, and the log swings across. Eli walks over the log, right above the bone, and stays tahor.

**Trap:** most kids will avoid the log because it's "over the bone". The level teaches that this is exactly right.

**Fail:** Eli steps on the seesaw → the far end lifts → the bone moves → Eli glows: *"Moving a bone counts like carrying it [משא]!"*

**Why? card**
> **EN:** A single bone from a person, even as small as a grain of barley [עצם כשעורה], makes you impure [טמא] if you **touch** it or **move** it. Even pushing down one end of a plank that the bone is lying on counts as moving it [הסט]. But a bone does **not** spread impurity under a roof [אהל], so walking **over** it is fine!
> **HE:** עצם אחת של אדם, אפילו כשעורה, מטמאה במגע ובמשא. גם הנדת קורה שהעצם מונחת עליה נחשבת כמשא (היסט). אבל עצם אחת אינה מטמאה באוהל, ולכן מותר לעבור מעליה!
>
> *"אֲפִלּוּ עֶצֶם כִּשְׂעוֹרָה מְטַמֵּא בְּמַגָּע וּבְמַשָּׂא... וְאֵינוֹ מְטַמֵּא בְּאֹהֶל"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 2:10) · *"קוֹרָה שֶׁהִיא מֻנַּחַת עַל הַכֹּתֶל... וּבָא הַטָּהוֹר לִקְצֵה הַקּוֹרָה הַשֵּׁנִי וְהֵנִידוֹ... טָמֵא מִשּׁוּם נוֹשֵׂא"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 1:7)

### 1-4 · The Gardener's Tree (העץ של הגנן)
**Teaches:** branches that spread over a grave are an ohel [אהל]. If the same branches also spread over the road, the road under them is tamei.
**New mechanics:** "free the helper" pin puzzle; pruning.

**Layout** (two rooms):
- **Room 1, the gardener's shed:** Gardener Gad is stuck behind a door held by two pins. A beehive hangs above one pin, and a water barrel sits above the other. Pulling the wrong pin first drops the hive onto the path, and the bees chase Gad back in (soft fail).
- **Room 2, the road:** a big old terebinth tree grows beside a grave. Its thick branches stretch over the grave **and** across the road. The shaded part of the road is tinted light purple (ohel).

**Solution:**
1. Pull the barrel pin first: water splashes the hive's spot so the bees stay home. Then pull the door pin. Gad comes out.
2. Tap the connecting branch. Gad climbs up and saws off the branch that reaches over the road. The purple tint vanishes from the road, and Eli walks through.

**Fail:**
- Eli walks under the branch → *"The branch is a roof over the grave AND the road. One roof [אהל] = impure [טמא] underneath!"*
- Sawing the wrong branch, one that isn't over the road, does nothing.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** A dead person [מת] spreads impurity to everything under the same roof [אהל]. A roof doesn't have to be a house: strong tree branches count too! When the branches of one tree cover both a grave [קבר] and the road, a Kohen [כהן] may not walk under them. Cutting off the branch over the road fixes it.
> **HE:** מת מטמא כל מה שתחת אותו אוהל. אוהל אינו רק בית: גם ענפי אילן חזקים נחשבים אוהל! אם ענפי אילן אחד מסככים גם על קבר וגם על הדרך, אסור לכהן לעבור תחתיהם. כשכורתים את הענף שמעל הדרך, הדרך טהורה.
>
> *"שָׂרִיגֵי הָאִילָנוֹת הַסּוֹכְכִים עַל הָאָרֶץ... אִם יְכוֹלִין לְקַבֵּל מַעֲזִיבָה בֵּינוֹנִית... הֲרֵי אֵלּוּ מְבִיאִין וְחוֹצְצִין מִן הַתּוֹרָה"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 13:2) · *"טֻמְאַת אֹהֶל... בְּמֵת בִּלְבַד"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 1:10)

**Guard-rail:** all branches are drawn thick and strong. Thin branches (rabbinic ohel) come in a later level.

### 1-5 · Stones That Don't Wobble (אבנים שאינן מתנדנדות)
**Teaches:**
- **Beit HaPeras [בית הפרס]**, part 1: what it is.
- Two ways to cross: solid stones, or riding a strong animal.
- Wobbly stones count as walking on the dirt itself, even if you don't wobble.

**New mechanics:** tap-test (tap a stone: solid ones go *thunk*, wobbly ones rock); choosing a ride.

**Layout:**
- A field with a white sign: *"Beit HaPeras: a grave was plowed here."* A deep stream on both sides, so there's no way around.
- Miriam is carrying the family's terumah [תרומה] basket, and she and Eli both need to stay tahor for it.
- Stepping stones cross the field. Some are big and solid, some small and wobbly.
- At the edge: two donkeys.
  - One is big and strong.
  - The other is a tired old donkey with knocking knees.

**Solution:**
- Tap-test the stones and draw a path on solid ones only, **or** put Eli and Miriam on the strong donkey (they can take turns: the donkey crosses, comes back, crosses again).
- Either way they reach the far side tahor.

**Fail:**
- Path includes a wobbly stone → Eli crosses perfectly balanced but still glows: *"A wobbly stone counts like the dirt itself, even if you didn't wobble!"*
- Choosing the weak donkey → it wobbles: *"A weak animal counts like walking on your own feet!"*

**Why? card**
> **EN:** A Beit HaPeras [בית הפרס] is a field where a grave was plowed by mistake, so tiny bone pieces might be scattered in the dirt. You stay pure [טהור] if you cross on **stones that don't wobble**, or **riding a strong person or animal**, so your feet never touch the dirt. But if the stones wobble, or the animal is weak, it's as if you walked on the dirt yourself.
> **HE:** בית הפרס הוא שדה שנחרש בו קבר, ועצמות קטנות אולי מפוזרות בעפר. המהלך על אבנים שאינן מתנדנדות, או רוכב על אדם או בהמה שכוחם יפה, טהור. אבל אם האבנים מזדעזעות, או שהבהמה חלשה, הרי זה כאילו הלך ברגליו.
>
> *"הַמְהַלֵּךְ בְּבֵית הַפְּרָס עַל גַּבֵּי אֲבָנִים שֶׁאֵין מִתְנַדְנְדִין... אוֹ... רוֹכֵב עַל גַּבֵּי אָדָם וּבְהֵמָה שֶׁכֹּחָן יָפֶה. הֲרֵי זֶה טָהוֹר"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 10:11; Mishnah Oholot 18:6)

### 1-6 · The Leaf-Blower Field ⭐ (שדה המפוח)
**Teaches:**
- **Beit HaPeras**, part 2.
- *"מנפח אדם בית הפרס והולך"*: you may blow on the ground to reveal tiny bones and walk around them.
- Tiny bones only count by touch and moving, not by ohel.
- This check is good enough for going to bring the Korban Pesach, but **not** for terumah.

**New mechanics:** the leaf blower (aim + hold); drawing a path on tiles.

**Layout** (two rooms):
- **Room 1, Gad's cart:** Gad's leaf blower is in his cart, up high on a golden pin. Another pin holds back a pile of hay on a ledge. Pull the hay pin first so the hay makes a soft landing, then the cart pin. If the cart drops first, it crashes. Gad hands **Eli** the blower: *"You check the ground yourself; that's how the Sages said to do it."*
- **Room 2, the field:**
  - Another plowed-grave Beit HaPeras. This one has no stones and no donkey; Miriam and the terumah went around the long way.
  - Eli is on his way to bring the **Korban Pesach** (it's the 13th of Nisan), and this is the only way to get there in time.
  - The field is a grid of dirt tiles. Some hide a tiny bone chip: cartoon, glowing faintly purple once revealed. One or two **bigger bones** are visible from the start.

**Play:**
1. Aim the blower in short gusts. Each gust clears a fan of tiles and reveals any chip hidden there.
2. Draw a path from start to finish on revealed, empty tiles.
3. Press "Walk".

**Solution:** any connected path through revealed, empty tiles. Limited "air" per level encourages planning: blow only where you mean to walk.

**Fail:**
- Path crosses a tile that was never blown: Eli steps on a hidden chip → glows → *"You didn't check that tile!"*
- Path passes right beside a chip: no fail, since there's no ohel. Kids learn they can walk close by.
- Blowing never pushes a chip onto the path or toward Eli.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** In a Beit HaPeras [בית הפרס], a bone the size of a barley grain only makes you impure [טמא] if you **touch it or move it**. It doesn't spread under a roof [אהל]. And the whole Beit HaPeras rule is from the Sages [מדרבנן]. So the Sages said: **blow on the ground in front of you as you walk**. Any tiny bone will show, and you step around it. Even a Kohen [כהן] may do this, and someone on his way to bring the Korban Pesach [קרבן פסח] may then bring it.
> But this check isn't good enough for eating terumah [תרומה]. That's why Miriam took the terumah around the long way.
> **HE:** בבית הפרס, עצם כשעורה מטמאה רק במגע ובמשא, ולא באוהל, וכל דין בית הפרס מדרבנן. לכן אמרו חכמים: מנפח אדם בית הפרס והולך. אם יש עצם קטנה, היא מתגלה, והוא עוקף אותה. גם כהן רשאי לעשות כך, ומי שהולך לעשות פסח שוחט ואוכל את פסחו. אבל לתרומה אין סומכים על בדיקה זו, ולכן מרים הקיפה עם התרומה.
>
> *"מְנַפֵּחַ אָדָם בֵּית הַפְּרָס וְהוֹלֵךְ"* (Berakhot 19b) · *"נְתָנוֹ בְּבֵית הַפְּרָס הֲרֵי זֶה עֵרוּב וַאֲפִלּוּ הָיָה כֹּהֵן... שֶׁיְּנַפֵּחַ וְהוֹלֵךְ"* (Rambam, Eruvin 6:17) · *"מִי שֶׁבָּא בְּבֵית הַפְּרָס הֲרֵי זֶה מְנַפֵּחַ וְהוֹלֵךְ... שׁוֹחֵט וְאוֹכֵל פִּסְחוֹ"* (Rambam, Korban Pesach 6:8) · *"בּוֹדְקִים לְעוֹשֶׂה פֶסַח, וְאֵין בּוֹדְקִין לִתְרוּמָה"* (Mishnah Oholot 18:4)

**Guard-rails:**
- The sign says *plowed* grave.
- Eli has a mitzvah purpose and no other road (Rambam Avel 3:14).
- Eli does the blowing himself.
- No terumah crosses this way.

### 1-7 · The Washed-Away Mark (הציון שנמחק)
**Teaches:**
- Graves in doubtful places are marked with lime [סיד] so people don't walk over them.
- The mark goes **a little wider** than the tumah (so pure things aren't ruined), but **not much wider** (so good land isn't wasted).
- Anyone who finds such a place must mark it.

**New mechanic:** painting with a lime brush (drag).

**Layout:**
- Winter rains washed away the lime marks along the road. Batya, the Beit Din's messenger, hands Eli a bucket.
- Grandpa Zevulun points out each spot, and a faint dotted outline appears where the tumah is. Three spots:
  - **(a)** a field where a grave was lost;
  - **(b)** a big branch over a grave;
  - **(c)** a stone jutting out of a wall over a grave.

**Play:** paint a lime outline around each dotted area, then press "Let the pilgrims through". A crowd of travelers walks the road.

**Scoring:** the outline must cover the whole tumah plus a small margin. The game shows a green band of "good distance".
- **Too tight:** a traveler's pure bag brushes the edge and glows → *"Leave some extra room!"*
- **Too wide:** a farmer runs out: *"You painted half my wheat field!"* → *"Not too far, either!"*

**Why? card**
> **EN:** Before the festivals, the Beit Din [בית דין] marked places of tumah with white lime [סיד], so travelers wouldn't become impure [טמא] by accident. The mark goes a little **beyond** the tumah, so nobody's pure things get ruined, but **not too far**, so good land in Eretz Yisrael isn't wasted. And anyone who finds a grave must mark it.
> **HE:** לפני הרגלים יצאו שלוחי בית דין לציין את מקומות הטומאה בסיד, כדי שעוברי דרכים לא ייטמאו. הציון עודף מעט על מקום הטומאה, כדי שלא להפסיד את הטהרות, אבל אין מרחיקים אותו, כדי שלא להפסיד את ארץ ישראל. וכל המוצא קבר חייב לציין עליו.
>
> *"כָּל הַמּוֹצֵא קֶבֶר... חַיָּב לְצַיֵּן עָלָיו... וּבַמֶּה מְצַיְּנִין, בְּסִיד... יְהִי עוֹדֵף מִכָּאן וּמִכָּאן... שֶׁלֹּא לְהַפְסִיד אֶת הַטָּהֳרוֹת. וְאֵין מַרְחִיקִין... שֶׁלֹּא לְהַפְסִיד אֶת אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 8:9) · *"וּמְצַיְּנִין אֶת הַקְּבָרוֹת"* (Mishnah Shekalim 1:1)

**Note:** the Rambam says only *doubtful* spots are marked (a lost grave, branches, jutting stones), not known cemeteries. All three spots in the level are of those kinds.

### 1-8 · Read the White Marks (קרא את הסימנים)
**Teaches:** how to read lime markings. This is a logic level, laid out as a grid.

**Layout:** a top-down "map view" of four fields. Eli needs to say which tiles are safe before the group walks.
- **Field A:** one stone painted with lime.
- **Field B:** two stones with lime spread on the ground between them.
- **Field C:** two stones with lime only on top, and potsherds between them.
- **Field D:** all four edges of the field painted.

**Play:** tap each tile to toggle tahor / tamei, then "Check".

**Answer:**
| Field | What's tamei |
|---|---|
| A | Only under the stone |
| B | The area between the stones |
| C | Nothing. It's just a building's foundation |
| D | The whole field. The painted edges themselves are tahor |

D is the twist: the mark is never put far from the tumah, so marks on all four sides mean the whole inside is tamei.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** One marked stone: the tumah [טומאה] is under it. Two stones with lime between them: the tumah is between them. Lime only on top of two stones with broken pottery between: that's just a building, it's pure [טהור]. All four edges of a field marked: the edges are pure and **the whole field** is impure [טמא], because a mark is never put far from the tumah.
> **HE:** אבן אחת מצוינת: תחתיה טמא. שתיים וסיד ביניהן: ביניהן טמא. סיד על ראשיהן וחרס ביניהן: אין זה אלא בניין, וטהור. ארבעה מצרי השדה מצוינים: המצרים טהורים וכל השדה טמאה, שאין מרחיקים את הציון ממקום הטומאה.
>
> *"מָצָא אֶבֶן מְצֻיֶּנֶת תַּחְתֶּיהָ טָמֵא... מָצָא אַרְבָּעָה מְצָרֶיהָ מְצֻיָּנִין. הֵן טְהוֹרִין וְכָל הַשָּׂדֶה כֻּלָּהּ טְמֵאָה"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 8:11)

### 1-9 · Trees or No Trees? (יש אילנות או אין?)
**Teaches:**
- Not every marked field is the same.
- A marked field **with trees** is a plowed-grave field: Beit HaPeras, which tiny-bone rules and blowing can handle.
- A marked field **without trees** is a field where **a whole grave was lost**. It spreads tumah by ohel too, so a Kohen can't cross it at all: not by blowing, not on stones, not on a donkey.

**New mechanic:** choosing between tools. All the tools from 1-5 and 1-6 are offered, and most don't work.

**Layout:**
- A crossroads with two marked fields.
  - The left field has olive trees.
  - The right field is bare.
- The road to Yerushalayim continues on the far side of both fields.
- Tap a field, then choose: 💨 blow and walk, 🫏 ride the strong donkey, or 🚶 just walk.

**Solution:** the bare field is a lost-grave field, so don't cross it. Cross the **left** field with the blower (as in 1-6) or on the strong donkey.

**Fail:**
- Using the blower in the bare field → nothing is revealed, and Grandpa Zevulun calls out: *"No trees: a whole grave was lost here! Blowing won't help. The grave spreads tumah by ohel [אהל]."*
- Riding the strong donkey across → Eli glows when he passes over the hidden grave.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** When you find a marked field and don't know why it was marked: if it **has trees**, it's a field where a grave was plowed [בית הפרס], since you're allowed to plant there. If it has **no trees**, it's a field where a **whole grave was lost**. A lost grave spreads impurity by touch, carrying **and roof** [אהל], so blowing and stones don't help. A Kohen must go around.
> **HE:** מצא שדה מצוינת ואין יודע מה טיבה: אם יש בה אילן, בידוע שנחרש קבר בתוכה. אין בה אילן, בידוע שאבד קבר בתוכה, ושדה שאבד בה קבר מטמאה במגע ובמשא ובאוהל. לכן ניפוח ואבנים אינם מועילים, והכהן מקיף.
>
> *"אִם יֵשׁ בָּהּ אִילָן בְּיָדוּעַ שֶׁנֶּחְרַשׁ קֶבֶר בְּתוֹכָהּ. אֵין בָּהּ אִילָן בְּיָדוּעַ שֶׁאָבַד קֶבֶר בְּתוֹכָהּ"* (Rambam, Tumat Met 10:10) · Lost-grave field: *"מְטַמֵּא בְמַגָּע וּבְמַשָּׂא וּבְאֹהֶל"* (Mishnah Oholot 18:3) · Moed Katan 5b

**Note:** the Rambam adds that this tree rule is used only where an elder or Torah scholar is there to say so (10:10). Grandpa Zevulun is that elder.

### 1-10 · The Pilgrims' Path (שביל העולים)
**Teaches:** a path through a Beit HaPeras that many feet have pressed down [בית הפרס שנדוש] is tahor for someone bringing the Korban Pesach, since any tiny bone has surely been crushed.

**New mechanic:** reading footprints. Then a short victory walk into Yerushalayim.

**Layout:**
- The last field before Yerushalayim, crowded with pilgrims.
- Several paths cross the field.
  - One is pressed hard and smooth, covered in thousands of footprints.
  - The others are fresh furrows or lightly-used goat trails.

**Solution:** pick the trodden path. Eli walks it, the walls of Yerushalayim appear, the music swells, and World 1 is complete.

**Fail:** picking a lightly-used trail → *"Not trodden enough. A tiny bone might still be there!"*

**Why? card**
> **EN:** When thousands of people have walked on a path in a Beit HaPeras [בית הפרס], any tiny bone there has surely been crushed to dust. So a well-trodden path is pure [טהור] for someone going to bring the Korban Pesach [קרבן פסח].
> **HE:** בית הפרס שנדוש, שעברו עליו רבים, טהור לעושה פסח, כי אי אפשר שעצם כשעורה לא נשחקה תחת הרגליים.
>
> *"וְכֵן בֵּית הַפְּרָס שֶׁנִּדּוֹשׁ טָהוֹר לָעוֹשֶׂה פֶּסַח"* (Rambam, Korban Pesach 6:8) · *"בית הפרס שנידש טהור"* (Berakhot 19b; Ketubot 28b)

## Chanukah Pack: The Sealed Jug (פך השמן)

It's Kislev, in the days of the Chashmona'im. The Greek army has been driven out of Yerushalayim, but they left the Beit HaMikdash in a mess, and the oil they touched is all tamei. **Yehuda** (יהודה), a young Kohen with dark curly hair, helps the older Kohanim get the Mikdash ready and find pure oil for the Menorah.

| # | Level | New mechanic | Main rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-1 | The Stones of the Altar | Slide heavy stones | The Chashmona'im hid away the altar stones the Greeks had defiled |
| C-2 | They Searched (בדקו) | Lantern + magnifier | Only a jug with the Kohen Gadol's seal, hidden and unmoved, was pure |
| C-3 | Sealed Tight | Sort jars | A tightly sealed clay jar protects its contents (tzamid patil); stone protects everything; metal doesn't |
| C-4 | Oil Is a Liquid | Pipes / channels | Liquids become a "1" even from a "2"; wash your hands |
| C-5 | Pure Oil Run | Multi-room route | Bring new pure oil without tumah: graves, trees, Beit HaPeras |
| C-6 | Eight Nights | Light the Menorah | Why they searched for pure oil instead of using tamei oil |

### C-1 · The Stones of the Altar (אבני המזבח)
**Teaches:** after the war, the Chashmona'im put away the stones of the Mizbeach that the Greek kings had defiled [שקצום]. They stored them in a sealed chamber of the Beit HaMoked [בית המוקד] and built a new altar.
**New mechanic:** sliding heavy stones along rails and ramps (pin puzzle).

**Layout:**
- The Azarah courtyard, cut-away view.
- Big cracked altar stones sit on a ledge.
- Below is a chamber in the north-east of the Beit HaMoked with its door open.
- The new altar's building site is on the other side, marked with rope.

**Solution:** pull pins in an order that slides every old stone into the chamber, and none onto the new altar's site. Then close the chamber door.

**Fail:** an old stone rolls onto the new altar's site → *"Those stones can't be used for the new Mizbeach!"*

**Why? card**
> **EN:** The Greek kings used the Mizbeach [מזבח] for idol worship, so its stones could never be used again. The Chashmona'im [חשמונאים] stored them away in a special chamber in the Beit HaMoked [בית המוקד] and built a new Mizbeach.
> **HE:** מלכי יוון שיקצו את אבני המזבח, ולכן אי אפשר היה להשתמש בהן עוד. בני חשמונאי גנזו אותן בלשכה שבבית המוקד ובנו מזבח חדש.
>
> *"מִזְרָחִית צְפוֹנִית, בָּהּ גָּנְזוּ בְנֵי חַשְׁמוֹנַאי אֶת אַבְנֵי הַמִּזְבֵּחַ שֶׁשִּׁקְּצוּם מַלְכֵי יָוָן"* (Mishnah Middot 1:6) · Avodah Zarah 52b

### C-2 · They Searched (בדקו)
**Teaches:**
- The Greeks made all the oils tamei. The Sages ruled that anything the Greek soldiers handled, or even just moved, was tamei.
- The Chashmona'im **searched** and found one jug lying with the **Kohen Gadol's seal** [חותמו של כהן גדול]. It was hidden away, so they knew no one had touched it.

**New mechanics:** a lantern (reveals clues in dark corners) and a magnifier (inspects seals).

**Layout** (a dark storeroom under the Heichal, three stacked rooms):
- Dozens of oil jugs. Clues on them:
  - smashed;
  - open, with no seal;
  - sealed, but the seal is broken;
  - sealed, but a drag mark in the dust shows it was moved.
- One jug sits buried in a niche behind loose stones in the lowest room.

**Play:**
1. Pull pins to drop crates and open a way down.
2. Use the lantern and magnifier to inspect jugs (tap to inspect).
3. Each jug gets a stamp: "touched / moved / not sure / **pure**".

**Solution:** the hidden jug in the niche: Kohen Gadol's seal intact, dust undisturbed.

**Fail:** choosing any other jug → a sage gently explains what its clue meant.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** When the Greeks entered the Heichal [היכל], they made all the oil impure [טמא]. The Chashmona'im **searched** and found just one jug lying with the Kohen Gadol's seal [חותמו של כהן גדול]. It was hidden and sealed, so they knew nobody had touched it, and it was pure [טהור]. It only had enough oil for one day.
> **HE:** כשנכנסו יוונים להיכל טימאו כל השמנים שבהיכל. בדקו ולא מצאו אלא פך אחד של שמן שהיה מונח בחותמו של כהן גדול, בהצנע, והכירו שלא נגעו בו. ולא היה בו אלא להדליק יום אחד.
>
> *"בָּדְקוּ וְלֹא מָצְאוּ אֶלָּא פַּךְ אֶחָד שֶׁל שֶׁמֶן שֶׁהָיָה מוּנָּח בְּחוֹתָמוֹ שֶׁל כֹּהֵן גָּדוֹל"* (Shabbat 21b) · Rashi there: *"בהצנע וחתום בטבעתו והכיר שלא נגעו בו"* · Moving, not only touching: Tosafot and Rashba on Shabbat 21b **[to verify wording]**

### C-3 · Sealed Tight (צמיד פתיל)
**Teaches:**
- In a room with a met, an open clay vessel's contents become tamei. A clay vessel sealed **tightly** [צמיד פתיל] keeps its food and drink pure.
- A sealed **stone** vessel protects **everything** inside, even a metal object.
- A sealed clay vessel protects only food, drink and clay. A **metal** vessel doesn't protect at all, sealed or not.

**New mechanic:** sorting items onto "Take to the Mikdash" / "Leave" shelves.

**Story:** a flashback. Before the war, a family hid their things from the Greeks in an old burial cave, a place the soldiers avoided. A Yisrael helper goes in to bring the things out; Yehuda, a Kohen, stays outside and sorts.

**Items:**
| Item | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Clay jar of oil, open | ✗ | Open clay vessel in an ohel with a met |
| Clay jar of oil, cloth tied loosely on top | ✗ | Not tightly sealed |
| Clay jar of oil, sealed with clay plaster | ✓ | Tzamid patil protects food and drink |
| Clay jar, sealed, holding only a **metal ladle** | ✗ | Clay only protects food, drink and clay vessels |
| **Stone** jar, sealed, with a metal ladle inside | ✓ both | Stone protects everything |
| **Metal** flask of oil, sealed | ✗ | Metal doesn't protect |
| Stone cup, open, empty | ✓ | Stone never becomes tamei |

**Why? card**
> **EN:** In a room with a dead person [אהל המת], everything becomes impure [טמא]. But a clay [חרס] vessel that is **sealed tight** [צמיד פתיל] keeps the food and drink inside it pure [טהור]. A sealed **stone** [אבן] vessel protects everything inside, even metal. A metal vessel doesn't protect at all.
> **HE:** באוהל המת הכול נטמא. אבל כלי חרס המוקף צמיד פתיל מציל על האוכלים והמשקים שבתוכו. כלי אבנים מציל על הכול, אפילו על כלי מתכת. כלי מתכת אינו מציל.
>
> *"וְכֹל כְּלִי פָתוּחַ אֲשֶׁר אֵין־צָמִיד פָּתִיל עָלָיו טָמֵא הוּא"* (Bamidbar 19:15) · *"אֵלּוּ כֵלִים מַצִּילִין בְּצָמִיד פָּתִיל... כְּלֵי אֲבָנִים... כְּלֵי חֶרֶס... עַל הַכֹּל מַצִּילִין, חוּץ מִכְּלֵי חֶרֶס, שֶׁאֵינוֹ מַצִּיל אֶלָּא עַל הָאֳכָלִים וְעַל הַמַּשְׁקִין וְעַל כְּלֵי חָרֶס"* (Mishnah Kelim 10:1) · Rambam, Tumat Met 21:1

**Guard-rail:** Yehuda never enters the cave. It's shown from outside, with the items carried out to him.

### C-4 · Oil Is a Liquid (שמן הוא משקה)
**Teaches:**
- Oil is one of the seven liquids [שבעה משקין].
- A liquid that touches something tamei becomes a **"1"** [ראשון], even if what it touched was only a "2" [שני]. With liquids, the counting doesn't go down.
- Hands count as a "2" by Rabbinic law until they are washed [נטילת ידיים].

**New mechanics:** channels and pipes. Oil flows downhill when a pin is pulled, and a hand-washing station appears.

**Layout:** the olive press. New oil flows from the press through stone channels down to the jars. On the way:
- **(a)** a worker with unwashed hands (his hands show a "2" badge) waits to steady a jar;
- **(b)** a loaf marked "2" sits next to one channel;
- **(c)** a washing station with a cup.

**Solution:**
1. Pull pins so the oil takes the channel away from the "2" loaf.
2. Send the worker to wash his hands first (tap the washing station), then let him steady the jar.

**Fail:** oil touches the "2" loaf → the oil turns "1" → *"Liquids always become a '1', even from a '2'!"*

**The aha:** kids expect a "2" to be too weak to matter. With liquids it isn't.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** Oil is a liquid [משקה]. Liquids are special: if a liquid touches anything impure [טמא], even something weak like a "second" [שני], the liquid becomes a strong "first" [ראשון]. That's why workers wash their hands [נטילת ידיים] before touching pure oil. Unwashed hands count as a "second".
> **HE:** שמן הוא משקה. משקין שנגעו בטומאה, אפילו בשני, נעשים ראשון. לכן הפועלים נוטלים ידיים לפני שנוגעים בשמן טהור, כי ידיים סתם הן שניות.
>
> *"הַמַּשְׁקִין... שֶׁנָּגַע בְּאַב הַטֻּמְאָה אוֹ שֶׁנָּגַע בְּרִאשׁוֹן אוֹ בְשֵׁנִי... הֲרֵי הוּא רִאשׁוֹן"* (Rambam, She'ar Avot HaTumot 7:5) · *"כָּל הַפּוֹסֵל אֶת הַתְּרוּמָה, מְטַמֵּא אֶת הַמַּשְׁקִין לִהְיוֹת תְּחִלָּה"* (Mishnah Parah 8:7) · Hands: Rambam, She'ar Avot HaTumot 8:1 · Oil among the seven liquids: Rambam, Tumat Okhalin 1:2

**Halachic note (learning mode):** liquids passing tumah on is Rabbinic, per the Rambam (She'ar Avot HaTumot 7:1-2).

### C-5 · Pure Oil Run (מסע השמן הטהור)
**Teaches:** review of World 1, plus the story: they needed new pure oil, and it took 8 days to bring it. One opinion (the Ran, quoted by the Maharsha) is that pure oil was 4 days away, so 4 days there and 4 back.

**Mechanics:** everything from World 1. A day counter at the top shows how many nights the little jug has burned so far.

**Layout** (a long, five-room route):
1. A grave by the road: take the far path (1-1).
2. A tree over a grave and the road: prune (1-4).
3. A plowed-grave Beit HaPeras: cross on solid stones or the strong donkey. **Not** by blowing, because this oil is for the Mikdash and blowing only clears the way for the Korban Pesach (1-5, 1-6).
4. A marked bare field: go around (1-9).
5. The press, washing station and sealed clay jars (C-3, C-4).

**Fail:** any rule from World 1, with the same hints.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** The one jug had oil for just one day, but making new pure oil took eight days. One explanation: the closest pure oil was four days away, so four days there and four days back. And the little jug burned for all eight days!
> **HE:** בפך היה שמן ליום אחד בלבד, ולא היה אפשר לתקן שמן טהור עד שמונה ימים. כתב הר"ן שהשמן הטהור היה רחוק ארבעה ימים. ונעשה נס והדליקו ממנו שמונה ימים!
>
> *"וְהִדְלִיקוּ מִמֶּנּוּ נֵרוֹת הַמַּעֲרָכָה שְׁמוֹנָה יָמִים עַד שֶׁכָּתְשׁוּ זֵיתִים וְהוֹצִיאוּ שֶׁמֶן טָהוֹר"* (Rambam, Hilchot Chanukah 3:2) · *"וכמ"ש הר"ן שהיה להם שמן רחוק ד' ימים"* (Maharsha, Chidushei Agadot, Shabbat 21b)

### C-6 · Eight Nights (שמונה לילות)
**Teaches:**
- **Why look for pure oil at all?** For the whole community [ציבור], tumah [טומאה] can be pushed aside when there's no choice. According to the Rambam, it's only *pushed aside* [דחויה], not truly permitted. So first you must **search** for a pure way, and only if there truly isn't one may you use tumah.
- That's why they searched, and Hashem made the miracle.

**New mechanic:** the finale. Light the Menorah each night and watch the oil level stay full.

**Play:**
- **Night 1:** a choice screen: "Use the impure oil" / "Search for pure oil first". Choosing to search replays a quick montage of C-2.
- **Nights 1–8:** light the Menorah. The jug's oil gauge never drops. Each night adds a light to a little Chanukiah in the corner of the screen.

**Why? card**
> **EN:** When the whole Jewish people [ציבור] is impure [טמא], the Avodah can still be done with impurity. But according to the Rambam, impurity is only **pushed aside** [דחויה] then, not truly permitted, so you must first **search** for a pure way. That's why the Chashmona'im searched for pure oil. And Hashem showed His love by making the one little jug last for eight days.
> **HE:** טומאה דחויה בציבור ואינה הותרה. לכן צריך לחזר תחילה אחר דרך טהורה, ורק במקום שאי אפשר דוחים. לכן בדקו החשמונאים אחר שמן טהור, והקב"ה הראה את חיבתו לישראל ועשה נס והדליקו ממנו שמונה ימים.

# Part C: idea bank for later worlds

Each idea: the rule it teaches, then the puzzle, then sources. Ideas marked ✦ are especially strong.

## World 2: The House of Rooms (ohel physics; the "old man through the rooms" format)
1. ✦ **One-Tefach Windows.**
   - Rule: tumah passes between rooms only through an opening of at least 1×1 tefach.
   - Puzzle: rooms are stacked like a dollhouse. Slide shutters to make windows smaller than a tefach (use the tefach gauge) so Eli's route stays clean. *Rambam Tumat Met 14:1.*
2. ✦ **The Door Must Stand by Itself.**
   - Rule: a door a person holds shut doesn't block tumah; a door wedged with a stone does.
   - Puzzle: Gad offers to "hold the door". That fails, and the player must find a wedge. *Rambam Tumat Met 18:3; Oholot 6:1.*
3. **Which Door Will They Use?**
   - Rule: in a closed house with a met, every doorway is tamei; opening (or choosing) the one the met will be carried out through saves the others.
   - Puzzle: pick the exit door. *Rambam Tumat Met 7:2; Oholot 7:3.*
4. ✦ **Tumah Laser.**
   - Rule: tumah packed tight with no 1-tefach air pocket goes straight up and down, not sideways.
   - Puzzle: slide crates; only those in the vertical beam get tamei. *Rambam Tumat Met 7:4-5.*
5. **The Drain Under the House.**
   - Rule: "tumah goes out, not in". A covered drain under a house is tahor when the tumah is in the house; tumah in the drain makes the house tamei unless the drain's exit is a tefach wide.
   - Puzzle: pipes. *Mishnah Oholot 3:7.*
6. **Thin Branches.**
   - Rule: weak branches bring tumah (Rabbinic) but don't block it.
   - Puzzle: a dashed-outline mechanic in learning mode. *Rambam Tumat Met 13:2.*
7. **The Board on the Donkey.**
   - Rule: people and vessels can't act as a blocking ohel, but a board resting on a live animal can.
   - Puzzle: build a protective roof. *Oholot 6:1; Rambam Tumat Met 13:3.*
8. **Stone, Clay or Metal?**
   - Rule: which vessels become tamei and how: stone never; clay only from inside; metal from any touch.
   - Puzzle: a quick-sort mini-game. *Rambam Kelim 1:6, 13:1.*

## World 3: The Kitchen (Rishon, Sheni, Shlishi)
1. ✦ **The Mouse in the Clay Pot.**
   - Rule: a sheretz in a clay pot's airspace makes everything inside tamei with no touch; rubbing the outside does nothing.
   - *Vayikra 11:33; Rambam Kelim 13:1.*
2. ✦ **The Bone-Dry Apple.**
   - Rule: food that was never wetted on purpose can't become tamei. An apple washed yesterday (now dry) can.
   - *Rambam Tumat Okhalin 1:1-2; Makhshirin 6:4.*
3. **Count the Hops.**
   - Puzzle: label each step of a chain: sheretz → oven → bread → terumah cake.
   - *Rambam Tumat Met 5:8; She'ar Avot HaTumot 11:2.*
4. ✦ **Three Plates.**
   - Rule: one "2" loaf touches a plain plate (nothing), a gold-rim terumah plate (3, pasul) and a blue kodesh plate (3, still passes it on).
   - *Rambam She'ar Avot HaTumot 11:2-4.*
5. **A Rishon Can't Tag You.**
   - Rule: a "1" high-fives a friend and grabs a spoon. Neither becomes tamei by Torah law; only his hands become a "2".
   - *Rambam Tumat Met 5:7; She'ar Avot HaTumot 8:1.*
6. ✦ **The Power-Up Drink.**
   - Rule: a "2" loaf drips into water, the water becomes "1", and it makes the salad "2".
   - *Rambam She'ar Avot HaTumot 7:5.*
7. **Too Small to Spread.**
   - Rule: a tamei crumb smaller than an egg's volume (k'beitzah, כביצה) can't pass tumah on. Add a second crumb and it can, at the weaker level of the two.
   - *Rambam Tumat Okhalin 4:1, 4:12.*
8. **Tamei vs. Pasul.**
   - Rule: grey "broken" items vs. pulsing "contagious" items. Build a buffer so terumah is never touched.
   - *Rambam She'ar Avot HaTumot 11:1.*
9. **Inside or Outside?**
   - Rule: tamei water splashes the *outside* of a pot. For terumah only the outside is tamei; for kodesh the whole pot is.
   - *Rambam She'ar Avot HaTumot 7:3, 7:6.*
10. **The Plain Steak.**
    - Rule: a perfectly tahor ordinary steak makes korban meat pasul. A trick level with nothing tamei in sight.
    - *Rambam She'ar Avot HaTumot 11:5.*

## World 4: Mikveh & Sunset
1. ✦ **Fill the Mikveh.**
   - Rule: dig channels so rainwater flows in by itself, 40 se'ah [ארבעים סאה], one amah by one amah by three amot. Water carried in a bucket doesn't count.
   - Learning mode: by Torah law even drawn water works (Rambam Mikvaot 4:1). Disqualifying it is a Rabbinic rule.
   - *Rambam Mikvaot 4:1-2 [4:2 to verify]; Mishnah Mikvaot 1.*
2. **Wait for the Stars.**
   - Puzzle: move clouds aside so the three stars can be seen.
   - *Rambam Terumot 7:2.*
3. **Tevul Yom vs. Dirty Hands.**
   - Rule: someone who went to the mikveh today (tevul yom, טבול יום) doesn't make plain soup tamei, but unwashed hands make terumah pasul.
   - *Mishnah Tevul Yom 2:2; Rambam She'ar Avot HaTumot 10:3.*
4. **Day 3 and Day 7.**
   - Rule: someone tamei from a met is sprinkled with Parah Adumah water on day 3 and day 7, then goes to the mikveh.
   - Puzzle: a calendar puzzle with several characters.
   - *Bamidbar 19:12, 19.*
5. **The Gates.**
   - Rule: a tevul yom may pass the Cheil but not enter Ezrat Nashim until sunset.
   - Puzzle: a gate-check mini-game, which is also the finale's mechanic.
   - *Mishnah Kelim 1:8.*

## World 5: The Bikurim Parade (Shavuot)
1. **Tie the Reed.**
   - Rule: mark the first fig, grape and pomegranate with a reed.
   - *Mishnah Bikurim 3:1.*
2. ✦ **Camp in the Square.**
   - Rule: the procession sleeps in the open square, not in houses, so no one is under a roof with a met.
   - Puzzle: place bedrolls; awnings and balconies count as roofs.
   - *Rambam Bikurim 4:16; Oholot 8:2.*
3. **The Golden-Horned Ox.**
   - Puzzle: lead the parade past graves, trees and a Beit HaPeras. A combination level.
   - *Mishnah Bikurim 3:2-3.*
4. **The King Carries His Basket.**
   - Story: King Agrippas carries his own basket into the Azarah. A gentle story level.
   - *Mishnah Bikurim 3:4.*

## World 6: Children of the Rock (Parah Adumah)
1. ✦ **Courtyard on Hollow Rock.**
   - Rule: build over a hollow space so a hidden deep grave [קבר התהום] can't reach the children.
   - *Mishnah Parah 3:2; Rambam Parah Adumah 2:7.*
2. ✦ **Ox Express to the Shiloach.**
   - Rule: ride on a door on an ox's back holding a stone cup.
   - Puzzle: a "lean meter", since leaning out past the door could make the child tamei.
   - *Parah 3:2; Sukkah 21a.*
3. **Stone Cups Only.**
   - Puzzle: choose stone, clay or metal cups for drawing the water.
   - *Parah 3:2; Rambam Kelim 1:6.*
4. **Seven Days Apart.**
   - Rule: the Kohen who burns the Parah is separated seven days and sprinkled.
   - *Mishnah Parah 3:1 [to verify details].*

## Pesach pack
1. ✦ **Pesach Sheni Sort.**
   - Rule: who brings the Pesach on 14 Nisan and who on 14 Iyar. A sheretz-tamei person goes to the mikveh and brings it now; a met-tamei person whose 7th day is today goes to Pesach Sheni.
   - *Rambam Korban Pesach 6:2; Bamidbar 9.*
2. **"Why Should We Miss Out?"** (למה נגרע)
   - Story: the people who were tamei met ask Moshe and receive Pesach Sheni.
   - *Bamidbar 9:6-11.*
