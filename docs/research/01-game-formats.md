# Research: "Ad-genre" rescue puzzle formats, for a kids' side-view puzzle game on the laws of ritual purity

Researched 2026-10-25. Every factual claim has a source URL next to it. Where I give numbers (level length, how many pins per level, and so on), they are my own estimates from the screenshots and genre conventions. They are marked **(est.)**.

Reference screenshots (App Store images) were looked at during research but are **not committed**, since they are other studios' copyrighted art. Section 5 describes each one and links to the original.

## 1. Game #1: "Knight saves the princess and gets the treasure; move/pull metal bars so you can walk through"

### Identification (high confidence: this is the "pull-the-pin" genre)

The description matches the pull-the-pin genre exactly. In the ads, the "metal bars" are golden pins (rods with a ring handle). The genre has two layers:

**A. The fake-gameplay ads that made it famous (2019–2021):**
- **Hero Wars (Nexters)** is credited with popularising pull-the-pin as an ad hook. Its ads showed a hero being guided past traps to treasure. Hero Wars and Evony both started using it in 2019, and both saw a big drop in cost-per-install. Lily's Garden and Angry Birds Dream Blast copied it later. Source: Pocket Gamer.biz, "The evolution of pull the pin" – http://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-evolution-of-pull-the-pin-why-is-this-a-good-thing-for-the-games-industry/
- Hero Wars' own fan guide confirms the pin game exists in the real game only as a side feature, "not a central gameplay element": https://h-w.fun/en/hero-wars-is-different-from-ads/?amp=1
- An Aftermath writer describes Hero Wars ads as "your little guy" moving sliders "in the right order to reach a weapon or treasure", with monsters, acid and arrows. A fake player deliberately pulls the wrong lever, which makes the viewer want to step in and solve it: https://aftermath.site/so-i-dont-totally-hate-those-hero-wars-ads.md
- **Evony: The King's Return**: Ars Technica-style coverage describes an Evony ad where the wrong pin "melts the gold and drowns the king". The UK ASA also ruled against a separate Evony ad (shooting numbered barrels) as not representative: https://asa.org.uk/rulings/top-games-inc-a23-1213924-top-games-inc.html
- **Homescapes / Gardenscapes (Playrix)**: the UK ASA banned these ads (ruling dated 30 Sept 2020). The Homescapes ad showed pins being pulled to bring a man and woman together without meeting a burglar ("Help her escape!"). The Gardenscapes ad showed a man, rising water, lava, money and a dog, with on-screen text **"HOW TO LOOT?"** and "ONLY 5% CAN SOLVE THIS". Source: https://asa.org.uk/rulings/plr-worldwide-sales-ltd-g20-1061644-plr-worldwide-sales-ltd.html ; Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homescapes ; TechRaptor: https://Techraptor.net/gaming/news/uk-asa-upholds-misleading-pin-pulling-puzzle-ad-claims-against-playrix
- **Royal Match (Dream Games)**: these "Save King Robert" ads are a related "save the character" format. They show a failed attempt at clearing tiles while the king is in danger. The real game has occasional "King's Nightmare" levels (roughly every 100 levels, per a blogger's testing): https://oldcynic.com/royal-match-save-the-king-levels-kings-nightmare ; https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/the-advertising-creative-trends-in-puzzle-games/

### The mechanic, precisely

1. **The level is a single screen**, normally in portrait. It shows a side cut-away of a tower, castle or house: stone walls, with chambers separated by walls and by **pins**.
2. **A pin** is a straight golden rod with a **ring handle** at one end. It sits in a slot through a wall. It can be horizontal, vertical or diagonal. Each pin works as a floor, a wall or a gate that holds something back.
3. **Input:** tap the pin, or swipe it in the direction its handle points. The pin slides out of the screen in about 0.2–0.4 s (est.). This is the *only* input in the classic version, and pins can't be put back. How To Loot adds pushing pins back in and rotating them, and treats them as "real physics objects with weight & friction" (App Store copy).
4. **Contents of chambers** are things that fall under gravity and interact:
   - **Gold, gems, coins**: pile up like particles and pour like sand. Collecting them is the goal or gives bonus stars.
   - **Water** (blue liquid) and **lava** (orange-red glowing liquid). **Water touching lava turns into stone/rock.** This is the signature combo: you use water to make lava safe before releasing it, or the reverse.
   - **Poison gas or acid** (bright green). It rises or flows. In some games it kills enemies but also the hero.
   - **Boulders and bombs**: fall and crush. Bombs explode.
   - **Enemies** (goblins, skeletons, monsters, burglar). They die from lava, rocks or gas, or the hero fights them (in some games only with a sword).
   - **The goal**: a princess, a treasure chest, or an exit door.
5. **The hero** is often **passive**. He waits until a path is open and then auto-walks along the floor to the goal or loot. In How To Loot he moves section by section: "Make a section of the hero's path safe, let him/her pass it, prepare the next section and so on, until the hero reaches the exit door" (App Store copy).
6. **The puzzle is about order.** Usually 2–5 pins (est.). Pulling the wrong one, or the right one at the wrong time, drops lava on the hero, lets the monster reach him, or melts the gold.
7. **Win:** hero reaches the princess, door or treasure. Typically there's a hug, hearts, a chest opening, coins flying to the HUD counter, confetti and a "LEVEL COMPLETE" banner. Stars: How To Loot gives 5 stars only if you collect *all* the loot.
8. **Lose:** the hero burns, is crushed or is caught. You see a skull, a cartoon "dead" pose (X eyes), and in some games a cry or scream. Then "Try again", usually with a rewarded-ad "Skip level".

## 3. Survey of related ad-ecosystem puzzle formats

For each format below: what you do, how you fail, how difficulty grows, how long a level takes, how it looks, how it feels (the win/lose effects), and the monetization to avoid. Level lengths are **(est.)** from the screenshots and genre knowledge.

### 3.1 Pull-the-pin (Hero Rescue / How To Loot / Pin Rescue / Pull Him Out)
- **Core mechanic:** remove pins in the right order so gravity sends hazards, loot and enemies where you want them. The hero then walks to the goal.
- **Input:** tap or swipe a pin's ring handle. Advanced versions also let you push pins back in or rotate them.
- **Fail states:** hero touches lava, gas, acid, a falling rock, a bomb or an enemy. Loot gets destroyed (gold in lava). The princess or pet dies. Sometimes there's a soft fail where you "win" without all the loot and get fewer stars.
- **Difficulty ramp:** levels 1–5 have one or two pins and one hazard. Then two hazards that interact (water + lava → stone). Then timing (pull one, wait for it to settle, pull the next). Then several rooms the hero walks through in stages (How To Loot), rotating pins, enemies that need a weapon, pipes that turn water into lava (How to Loot 2), and bosses. New elements come roughly every 5–10 levels (est.).
- **Level length:** 5–20 s of play once solved, 15–60 s with thinking (est.). Ads squeeze it to about 15 s.
- **Visual style:** portrait, a **single fixed camera** showing the whole level (no scrolling). One tall cut-away structure fills about 70–80% of the screen width. Walls are thick grey stone or flat pastel 3D. Pins are a saturated **gold/orange** that stands out from the muted walls. Characters are small, about **6–10% of screen height**, chunky and big-headed. Liquids are flat, very saturated colours: water blue, lava orange-red with glow, gas acid-green.
- **Juice:** the pin slides out with a "shink" sound. Particles pour. Steam puffs when lava hits water. Coins fly to the HUD counter. Confetti and stars on win. A tutorial hand points at the first pin.
- **Monetization to avoid:** an interstitial after almost every level. "Skip level" and "hint" buttons that play a video ad (with an **"Ads" badge right on the button**, see Save the Dog). Coin shop, "remove ads" IAP ($2.99–$4.99 for How To Loot), unlock-all bundles ($9.99 in Save Daddy). Fake-difficulty ads ("ONLY 5% CAN SOLVE THIS").

### 3.2 Draw-a-protective-shape: "Save the Doge / Save the Dog: Bee Draw Puzzle"
- **Core mechanic:** draw one continuous line that turns into a solid physical object. Then bees swarm out of hives for **10 seconds**. The dog must not be stung. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6443734068
- **Input:** one finger stroke. Ink is limited (shown by a pencil meter) and the line can't overlap the dog.
- **Fail:** the dog is stung (cries, swells). The shape collapses or falls over. You run out of ink.
- **Ramp:** one hive, then hives on both sides, then hazards like spikes, lava, bombs and water, then less ink.
- **Length:** about 5 s of drawing plus a fixed 10 s survival timer, so 15–20 s total.
- **Visual:** white **graph-paper** background. Small grassy dirt platforms. Cartoon pug head about 10% of screen width. Black marker strokes. 3-star ink meter at the top.
- **Juice:** wobbly physics on the drawn shape, buzzing bees, a countdown, a happy dog with hearts.
- **Avoid:** "Ads" badges on Skip and Hint buttons (visible in the screenshot), and a flood of clone apps.

### 3.3 Draw-a-path / bridge: "Love Balls", "Toilet Rush / Rush Home", "Draw to Home"
- **Core mechanic:** Love Balls: drawn lines become solid objects so two balls roll together (https://poki.com/en/g/love-balls). Toilet Rush: draw a path from each character to their goal and they run along it at the same time. Collisions with each other or with obstacles fail the level (https://www.crazygames.com/game/toilet-rush---draw-puzzle).
- **Input:** drag to draw.
- **Fail:** ball falls off screen. Characters collide. Character touches a hazard or a moving enemy.
- **Ramp:** fewer strokes or less ink for 3 stars ("draw as little as possible"). More characters at once. Moving obstacles. Love Balls adds daily challenges as a difficulty spike (https://megacool.medal.tv/blog/how-love-balls-became-a-viral-smash-and-6-ways-to-improve-it/).
- **Length:** 5–20 s (est.).
- **Visual:** minimalist. White or pastel background, black lines, a few bright accent colours.
- **Avoid:** Common Sense Media calls Love Balls "hampered by an over-abundance of ads": https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/love-balls

### 3.4 Rope-cutting: "Cut the Rope" (ZeptoLab), "Rescue Cut – Rope Puzzle"
- **Core mechanic:** swipe across ropes to cut them. Swinging weights, boxes or boulders then hit things, or the hero drops. In Rescue Cut a burning hero must be dropped into water, and there are pins, traps and enemies. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1566001569 ; https://www.xatakamovil.com/aplicaciones/asi-rescue-cut-rope-puzzle-sencillo-juego-cortar-cuerda-que-acumula-millones-descargas-ios-android
- **Input:** swipe gesture across a rope. Timing matters because things swing.
- **Fail:** wrong cut order, bad timing on the swing, hero falls into a pit or hazard, enemy survives.
- **Ramp:** one rope, then several ropes with timing, then chain reactions (a boulder knocks a crate that pushes a TNT box).
- **Length:** 5–15 s (est.).
- **Visual (Rescue Cut screenshot):** flat vector style. Pale sky-blue background, green ground blocks, cyan water, simple wooden crates, a grey wrecking ball on a black rope. A stick-figure hero about 8% of screen height. HUD has a menu button, retry, a **TIME: 600s** timer, a 3-star fuse bar, and a **HINT button with a "10" badge**.
- **Avoid:** heavy ads (the Spanish review mentions "a lot of ads"). The hint economy.

### 3.5 Rotate-the-room: "Beneath the Lighthouse" (Nitrome)
- **Core mechanic:** you don't control the character. You **rotate the whole room** (a circular frame) and gravity rolls the character through it. Levels are about 3 connected rooms with checkpoints and a timer. https://toucharcade.com/2015/10/26/what-is-nitromes-beneath-the-lighthouse-find-out-this-week ; https://www.mobygames.com/game/beneath-the-lighthouse
- **Input:** slide a finger in an arc to rotate.
- **Fail:** spikes, seagulls, crabs, running out of time. Reviewers say "you can overshoot to your death", so finger rotation can be fiddly.
- **Ramp:** each segment is harder than the last, and new enemy types are added.
- **Length:** 30–90 s per level (est.).
- **Visual (screenshot):** chunky **pixel art** in purple, brown and peach. A teal circular ring frames the playable room. White spikes. A small round white character (about 5% of screen width). A big curved arrow and pixel hand show the tutorial gesture.
- **Monetization:** free with ads plus lives. The premium unlock removes ads and lives. This is a reasonable model to copy if you need one.

### 3.6 "Choose the right item": "Save the Girl!" (Lion Studios), Brain Out, Hero Wars "pick the door/weapon" ads
- **Core mechanic:** the scene pauses at a danger. The player picks 1 of 2–3 items (scissors vs. jetpack vs. stairs). A wrong pick plays a comic, painful outcome. Later levels need several correct picks in a row. https://www.appgamer.com/save-the-girl-walkthrough-guide/ ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1490384223
- **Input:** tap one of the item cards.
- **Fail:** wrong item, followed by a funny failure animation.
- **Ramp:** from one decision to chains of 3–4. Answers that look obvious become trick answers.
- **Length:** 5–15 s per decision (est.).
- **Visual:** thick-outlined cartoon characters, large and about 30–40% of screen height, on a bright suburban side-view backdrop. Two item bubbles at the bottom.
- **Avoid:** roughly 50/50 guessing (a reviewer's point). Slapstick harm and **sexualised character design**, which Save the Girl has. Many interstitials.
- **Note:** this format maps very well onto **halachic decision questions** ("which vessel/item becomes tamei here?"). A wrong answer can show a gentle, non-violent consequence (the item turns grey, a "tamei" sparkle appears).

### 3.7 Dig-a-path: "Dig This!" (Raketspel)
- **Core mechanic:** your finger erases sand or dirt to carve a channel. Balls roll by physics into cups. Later: bombs, coloured balls matched to coloured cups, antigravity, breakable walls (Dig This 2). https://apps.apple.com/app/id1453411110 ; https://mwm.ai/apps/dig-this/1453411110
- **Fail:** balls end up in the wrong cup or get stuck. A rock blocks the path.
- **Ramp:** speed and collisions matter. More balls. Colour sorting.
- **Length:** 10–30 s (est.).
- **Visual (screenshot):** warm beige sand with a polka-dot texture. The dug channel is lavender. Glossy green 3D balls. A grey rock. Four green cups at the bottom inside glass bubbles. A big cartoon hand with a white cuff shows the digging gesture. Hint button with a count badge, and a retry button.
- **Juice:** sand crumbles in chunky particles. Balls clack into the cups.

### 3.8 Water / lava / sand flow (part of the pin games, and Fishdom ads)
- **Core mechanic:** liquids follow simple rules. Water + lava → stone. Water fills a pool, floats a boat, or rescues a fish. Gas rises. In the ASA's description, the Gardenscapes ad is entirely "rising water, lava, money".
- **Fail:** the wrong liquid reaches the character (lava burns, water drowns a non-swimmer, gas poisons). Loot is destroyed.
- **Teaching value:** this is a natural metaphor for **purification by immersion**: a mikveh fills with naturally gathered water, and contact rules decide whether something is still tamei or becomes pure. See section 4.

### 3.9 Sliding blocks / lever and door rooms (Merlin's Adventure, Pin Rescue's "dollhouse", Home Pin)
- **Core mechanic:** drag crates, boulders or cages. Flip levers and pressure plates that open doors. Guards and traps react. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/merlins-adventure-room-escape/id855207003
- **Pin Rescue look (screenshots):** flat pastel 3D "dollhouse". Cream or periwinkle walls with visible thickness. Gold pins with ring handles sticking out past the walls on both sides. Low-poly people, a boar and a tiger. Sky-blue or sage background. Bold black "LEVEL 28" / "GIRL RESCUE" title. Gear icon top-left, restart top-right, and a "skip (ad)" icon under it.

### 3.10 Monetization patterns seen across the ecosystem: AVOID for a kids' game
1. Forced interstitial video ads between levels. Ads in kids' apps are common and often manipulative (Radesky et al., JAMA Netw Open 2022: only about 1 in 5 preschool apps had *no* manipulative design): https://michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use
2. **Ad-gated hints and skips** ("Ads" badge on the Hint and Skip buttons).
3. **"Watch ad to triple coins"**, spin-the-wheel and loot boxes.
4. **Lives or energy timers** ("wait 30 min or pay").
5. **Fabricated urgency:** countdown offers, "ONLY 5% CAN SOLVE THIS", "Bumper Offer".
6. **Parasocial pressure:** a character cries or begs you to buy or keep playing.
7. Star gates that require replaying to grind before the next chapter. Keep stars as mastery, not as a paywall.
8. Misleading store creatives (the ASA rulings above).
9. Third-party ad SDKs and analytics. Apple's Kids Category (Guideline 1.3) requires a parental gate before links out, permission requests or purchases, and restricts third-party ads and analytics. Any ads must be human-reviewed for age appropriateness. https://developer-mdn.apple.com/kids/ ; https://www.goodbarber.com/help/apple-rejection-r100/apple-rejection-apps-for-kids-a365/
10. Gore, scream audio and sexualised characters (Save the Girl). Keep failure gentle.

## 4. Mechanics that map onto "move objects/pins/doors so a character reaches the goal without touching hazards"

These are format ideas only. The halachic content needs a rabbi or educator to review it.

| Mechanic (from the ads) | Kid-friendly purity version |
|---|---|
| **Pins/gates hold back a substance** | Gates hold back things that pass on tumah (impurity), shown as a grey glowing cloud or "mist" rather than lava. The character must reach the goal (e.g. the Beit HaMikdash courtyard or a mikveh) without touching it. |
| **Water + lava → stone** (neutralising a hazard by combining) | Let natural water flow into a pool until it reaches the required amount, then let the character immerse. Or a vessel touches the impurity-mist and turns grey, then is immersed and turns back. **Avoid** suggesting that water "kills" the impurity; teach the real rule. |
| **Hero walks on his own once the path is clear** (How To Loot, staged walk) | The kohen or child walks by himself. The player prepares each room in turn. This suits young kids because there's no dexterity requirement. |
| **Collect all loot for 5 stars** | Optional stars for doing everything in the halachically ideal order: right blessing, vessel immersed, not walking under a covering (*ohel*) that's over something impure. |
| **"Ohel" / roof mechanic** (new, fits the genre) | Movable roofs, awnings and doors. Tumah spreads to anything under the same roof as its source. The player slides awnings and opens doors so the character's route never shares a covered space with the source. This is a natural pin-pull variant: pulling a pin drops or removes a roof. |
| **Choose the right item** (Save the Girl) | "Which of these vessels can become impure?" (earthenware vs. metal vs. stone). Stone vessels don't receive tumah, which is great "trick answer" material. A wrong pick turns the item grey with a gentle "oops". |
| **Rotate the room** (Beneath the Lighthouse) | Tilt the room so a rolling vessel reaches the mikveh pool without touching the grey mist. |
| **Draw a protective shape** (Save the Doge) | Draw a lid or partition (*tzamid patil*, a sealed lid) over an earthenware jar before the mist reaches it. A sealed vessel in the tent stays pure. This is a strong, accurate halachic hook. |
| **Dig a path** (Dig This) | Dig a channel so *rainwater* flows naturally (not drawn by hand) into the mikveh. This teaches the *mayim she'uvim* (drawn water) concept. |
| **Rope cut / drop** | Drop a vessel into the mikveh by cutting its rope. A wrong cut drops it onto the impure spot. |
| **Levers and doors in multi-room cut-away** (Home Pin, How To Loot) | A side cut-away house or Temple courtyard in 2–4 stacked rooms, with the character moving room by room. |

**Recommended core loop:** a How To Loot-style **staged multi-room cut-away**, plus pin/door/roof toggles. The character auto-walks once a room is safe. Failure is soft and non-violent (the character gets a grey sparkle, says "Oops, I'm tamei now!", and the level rewinds in 1 s). Add an optional "explain why" card after the level.

### Format recipe a designer could reproduce (from the screenshots)
- **Canvas:** portrait 9:19.5. Top 10% for the HUD: level number in the centre, restart top-right, a soft-currency counter top-left. Bottom 10–15% empty or a banner.
- **Play area:** one cut-away structure about 80% of screen width, centred. Wall thickness about 3–4% of screen width. 2–4 chambers stacked vertically or in a diamond.
- **Pins:** one consistent high-contrast colour (gold or orange) with a large ring handle (diameter about 6–8% of screen width, so it's easy for small fingers to hit) that sticks out past the outer wall.
- **Characters:** 6–10% of screen height, big heads, readable silhouettes. The goal character stands in the farthest chamber.
- **Hazards:** one flat saturated colour per hazard type, each with a recognisable motion (lava glows and bubbles, water ripples, gas drifts upward).
- **Background:** a soft gradient sky or blurred scenery behind the structure, so the structure reads as a "dollhouse" cross-section.
